Studio Posing Guide

How to Look Relaxed & Authentic in Front of the Camera

Sarah Jenkins October 08, 2025 7 Min Read

Over 80% of our portrait clients begin their session by saying: "I'm completely unphotogenic and I hate having my picture taken." The truth is that being photogenic is not a genetic trait; it is simply the byproduct of relaxation, micro-angles, and guided breathing.

1. The "Exhale & Drop" Breathing Technique

When people feel self-conscious, their shoulders instinctively creep upward toward their ears, tensing the trapezius muscles and compressing the neck.

Before every key shutter click, take a deep diaphragmatic breath in through your nose, and as you exhale through your lips, consciously release your shoulder blades downward and backward. This creates an open chest posture that radiates effortless self-confidence.

The Peter Hurley "Squinch" Secret

Wide, deer-in-headlights eyes communicate fear or surprise. By slightly tightening only your lower eyelids while relaxing your upper brow, you introduce warmth, focus, and natural intrigue into your gaze.

2. The "Turtle" Neck Extension for Jawline Definition

In two-dimensional photography, facing the camera flat can obscure the separation between your jaw and neck. By pushing your forehead slightly toward the camera lens and tipping your chin down an inch (what photographers call "the turtle"), your jawline instantly sharpens while eliminating any trace of a double chin.

3. What to Do with Your Hands

Hands hanging limply look awkward. Give them a purpose:

  • Hook one thumb lightly into a trouser or blazer pocket while keeping four fingers relaxed.
  • Adjust a cufflink, wristwatch, or jacket lapel for dynamic editorial candids.
  • Rest hands gently on the armrest or across the lap with curved, soft fingers.
