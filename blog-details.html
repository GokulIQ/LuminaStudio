Top 7 Reasons Passport Photos Get Rejected (And How to Avoid Them)

Marcus Sterling October 14, 2025 5 Min Read

Submitting an international passport or visa application is already a tedious task. Having your application delayed by 6 to 10 weeks simply because your photo failed automated biometric scanning is both frustrating and costly.

1. Eyeglasses Glare and Thick Frames

For United States passports and DS-160 visa forms, the US Department of State strictly prohibits eyeglasses of any kind (even prescription lenses). For European, UK, and Canadian passports where glasses may be permitted for medical necessity, the reflection from flash strobes or thick frames obscuring the iris will cause immediate biometric failure.

Expert Studio Tip: We always recommend removing glasses for all passport sessions regardless of nationality to ensure guaranteed 100% automated acceptance.

2. Inappropriate Head Size & Proportions

According to ICAO Document 9303 standards, your face (measured from the bottom of the chin to the top of the head/crown) must occupy between 70% and 80% of the total vertical height of the image. Too zoomed out or too close will trigger an algorithmic rejection by embassy optical scanners.

3. Shadow Artifacts on the Face or Background

Smartphone photos taken in home hallways almost universally suffer from harsh overhead shadows cast beneath the nose and chin, or dark shadows projected onto the wall behind the head. Professional studio lighting uses multiple diffused strobes to fill all shadows evenly.
